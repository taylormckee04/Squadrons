Squadrons — Privacy Policy

Effective November 4, 2026

Squadrons is a browser game you can play at squadrons.onrender.com. This page explains, in plain language, what information the game touches and what happens to it. The short version: single player collects nothing at all, and multiplayer stores your account ID, the gamertag you choose, and the results and ratings of your queue matches.

Playing without signing in

Single player needs no account and no sign-in. The game runs entirely in your browser and sends no personal information anywhere.

Signing in with Google (multiplayer only)

Multiplayer asks you to sign in with Google so the game can tell players apart. When you sign in, Google shares three things with Squadrons: your name, your email address, and your profile picture, along with a Google account ID number that identifies your account. Squadrons never sees or receives your Google password — sign-in happens entirely on Google's side.

How that information is used

  • To confirm to the game server that you are really signed in (the server checks your sign-in with Google when you connect).
  • Your account ID number is the key your gamertag hangs on (see Storage below) — it is how the game knows which gamertag is yours when you come back.
  • To record the results of queue matches and keep your rating (see Storage below).

Your Google name, email address, and picture are never shown to other players. Other players see only your gamertag — the display name you choose yourself. Squadrons does not use your information for advertising, does not sell it, and does not share it with third parties for marketing.

Storage

Squadrons keeps a small accounts database (hosted by Neon, a Postgres database provider). For each signed-in player it stores:

  • Your Google account ID number (not your email address).
  • The gamertag you chose. Gamertags are unique — the database is also how the game makes sure two players can't hold the same name.
  • Your rating (a number that starts at 1000 and moves up or down as you play queue matches) and how many rated games you have played.

If you change your gamertag, the name you gave up is kept on a locked list for 30 days so nobody else can grab it the moment you drop it.

Queue match results are recorded. When a queue match ends, the game stores the result: where each player finished, the match statistics shown on the end-of-game screen (damage dealt, clouds earned, buildings and planes built and lost, and the match length), and each player's rating before and after. This is what makes ratings and the leaderboard possible. Custom games and single player are never recorded — only queue matches count. If the database is unreachable when a match ends, that match simply isn't recorded.

The public leaderboard page shows, for players with at least one rated game: their gamertag, their rating, how many rated games they have played, and how many they have won. The page also shows aggregate statistics per animal (how often each animal is picked by human players in rated queue matches, and how it places) — those numbers are totals across all players and identify nobody. Nothing else about you is public — never your name, email address, or account ID.

Your Google name, email address, and picture are held only in the game server's memory while you are connected, are never written to the database or saved to disk, and are discarded when you disconnect.

Cookies

Squadrons itself sets no tracking or advertising cookies. The Google sign-in process may set cookies on Google's own domains; those are governed by Google's Privacy Policy.

Children

Squadrons is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect information from them.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the new version will be posted on this page with a new effective date.

Contact
